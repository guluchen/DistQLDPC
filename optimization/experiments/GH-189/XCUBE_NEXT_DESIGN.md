## Independent finite disjoint-detector proof to check on actual matrices

General lemma: let a complete logical detector basis of the opposite half
be supplied. For EACH detector D, supply L pairwise support-disjoint
representatives in D's own stabilizer coset. Any tested nontrivial kernel
word W has odd pairing with some basis detector D; commuting with all
stabilizers makes its pairing odd with every one of the L representatives.
W must meet every disjoint support, so its physical weight>=L. A verified
logical word of weightL gives the matching upper. Necessary actual checks:
complete quotient/pairing ranks; every detector/repr kernel; exact coset
membership, physical support disjointness and nontrivial upper word.
This is finite GF2 algebra, not a support/quantum distance enumeration.

For cube-X12/plane-star-Z4 on periodic positive-axis links q=(axis,vertex),
X lines along one axis are in the Z-check kernel. Per orientation, propose
a full basis: lines at transverse(a=0,all b) plus lines at(a>0,b=0), total
2L-1. Z ribbons use parallel axis-i edges at one fixed axis-i layer, run
across one transverse direction and fix the other. Choose all fixed-b
ribbons(L) and fixed-a ribbons at a>0(L-1). The line/ribbon pairing per
orientation is block triangular with identity diagonal: first L lines
pair as delta_b with the first L ribbons, and remaining L-1 lines as
delta_a with the remaining ribbons. Verify this against actual columns.
Together3(2L-1)=6L-3 matches the independently computed k if valid.

For a Z ribbon detector, translating its fixed axis-i layer through all L
positions gives disjoint representatives. Adjacent layers differ by the
product of coplanar Z-star checks along the full ribbon direction. Verify
that actual stabilizer membership, not a drawing. Such a ribbon is a
transverse row of PARALLEL axis-i edges, not an ordinary connected path
of axis-i edges (which would anticommute with cube checks).

For an X line at transverse(a,b), choose a bijection between the L-1
other a' and L-1 other b' coordinates. For each pair(a',b'), use the XOR
of three lines(a',b),(a,b'),(a',b'). Its difference from the base line is
a four-corner rectangle of lines, supplied by the product of cube-X checks
across that transverse rectangle and the full periodic longitudinal axis.
The base line and these L-1 triple-line representatives are mutually
support-disjoint: distinct transverse rows/columns/diagonals do not meet.
Actual GF2 coset membership must verify the proposed geometric product.

Both detector families then give dX,dZ>=L, with actual line/ribbon upperL.
